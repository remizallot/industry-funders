Purpose

This list exists to help the School of Biological and Chemical Sciences find companies with a track record of paying for university research: PhD studentships, fellowships, Knowledge Transfer Partnerships, collaborative grants and co-funded publications. A company that has done this before, in our subject areas, is the most likely to do it again. Every row in the explorer is backed by a public record that can be checked.

Pipeline

Shapes follow the ISO 5807 flowchart convention: parallelograms are inputs, rectangles are processes, cylinders are stored data, and the curled boxes are deliverables.

How scope is decided

Scope is inclusion-first so that nothing in the School's remit (the former Life Sciences and Natural Sciences departments) is dropped at source; precision comes from scoring and from your filters. A GtR project is in scope if its lead funder is BBSRC, NERC or MRC, or a UKRI research subject or topic maps to a School theme, or a theme keyword matches the title. AHRC and ESRC projects need a subject match. An Innovate UK project is in scope if its sector is health and nutrition, life and land, life sciences, engineering biology, vaccines, agri-tech or energy and environment, or a theme keyword matches its title or public description. OpenAlex works are restricted to 70 subfields from molecular biology through ecology and earth science to materials chemistry. Universities, NHS bodies, research institutes, government agencies, charities and councils are recognised by name and removed from the company side; research and technology organisations and catapults are kept but flagged.

Scoring

Each evidence row earns points by type, scaled by recency over twenty years: studentship 3, fellowship 2, KTP 2, named partnership 2, research grant 1, other Innovate UK project 0.5. Publication rows earn 0.15 per paper funded and 0.05 per paper co-authored, capped at 60 and 20 points per company, so publication volume supports but never dominates the direct evidence of funded people. Companies are keyed on Companies House number where either source gives one, otherwise on a normalised name with legal suffixes removed and a short alias list (GSK, AstraZeneca, Unilever and similar). In the explorer, the score is recomputed from the rows that pass your year range and evidence-type filters.

Limitations

  • GtR records partner contributions as declared at award, not what was paid, and block doctoral training grants list the grant rather than every industrial student.
  • Innovate UK data shows that a company and a university were on the same project, not that a student or researcher was funded.
  • Company name matching is imperfect: subsidiaries and spelling variants can split one company into several rows. The aliases are shown when a row is expanded.
  • Themes are tagged by keyword and are broad; large companies match every theme.
  • Directly negotiated studentships that were never advertised and never produced an acknowledged paper are invisible to public data.

Rerunning the analysis at a later date

This page is a snapshot of public data as it stood on . The sources keep changing: Gateway to Research is refreshed periodically, UKRI updates the Innovate UK spreadsheets several times a year, and OpenAlex changes continuously. All sources are open or published under the Open Government Licence. The pipeline is eight scripts and four configuration files in the GitHub repository, with a Dockerfile and a one-command runner (scripts/run_all.sh); rerunning it rebuilds this page and the workbook from the current public data in about an hour, most of it the Gateway to Research download.

The subject scope is set in the configuration files and is specific to the School of Biological and Chemical Sciences. To produce a list for another school, or for the whole university, the theme keywords, UKRI subjects and OpenAlex subfields in config/themes.yml and config/openalex_subfields.yml would be changed and the pipeline rerun; the code itself needs no change. The original proposal, written for the School's Head of Research, is embedded here: download the proposal (PDF).
